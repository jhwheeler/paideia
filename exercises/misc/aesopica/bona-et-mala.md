# Natural Theology Hiding Behind Two Humble Letters

In one of Aesop's lesser known fables, we hear an origin story of the woes and boons that befall and bless mankind, quite different from the usual antics of talking animals that we see in the more popular fables. The fable recounts how the Evils drive away the Goods, which flee to Heaven, where they ask Zeus how they can go to be with people. Zeus tells them to go one by one, rather than all at once. In the original Greek, there is a nuance that is quite hard to translate and is usually simply ignored, which reveals to us the profound understanding of metaphysics and morality that the author was sharing with us.

> Ἀγαθὰ καὶ κακά
> Ἀγαθὰ πάντα ὑπὸ τῶν κακῶν ἐδιώχθη, ὡς ἀσθενῆ ὄντα· εἰς οὐρανὸν δὲ ἀνῆλθον. Καὶ τἀγαθὰ ἠρώτησαν τὸν Δία, πῶς εἶναι δεῖ μετὰ ἀνθρώπων. Ὁ δὲ εἶπε, μὴ μετʼ ἀλλήλων πάντα, ἓν δὲ καθʼ ἓν τοῖς ἀνθρώποις ἐπέρχεσθαι. Διὰ τοῦτο τὰ μὲν κακὰ συνεχῆ τοῖς ἀνθρώποις, ὡς πλησίον ὄντα, ἐπέρχεται, τὰ δὲ ἀγαθὰ βράδιον ἐξ οὐρανοῦ κάτεισι.
> Ὁ λόγος δηλοῖ, ὅτι ἀγαθῷ μὲν οὐδεὶς ταχέως ἐπιτυγχάνει, ὑπὸ δὲ τῶν κακῶν ἕκαστος καθʼ ἑκάστην πλήττεται.

> Goods and Evils
> The Evils drove away all the Goods, for they were seemingly weak; then the Goods went up to Heaven. The Goods asked Zeus how they should live with mankind. He told them not to go to people all at once, but rather one by one. This is why Evils are continually coming to people, seemingly being close by, while the Goods descend more slowly from Heaven. The story shows that no one happens quickly upon that which is Good, while everyone is struck by Evils every day.
